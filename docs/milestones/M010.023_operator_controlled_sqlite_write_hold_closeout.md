# Milestone M010.023 — Operator-Controlled SQLite Write Hold

## 1. Scope Completed

- Exercised an operator-controlled filesystem hold against a private disposable
  SQLite candidate only. No live service, production database or recovery

- Verified reads, online backup and separate restore under the hold; tested
  browser and independent CLI write denial with fresh coverage; verified
  unprivileged mode-change and parent-path replacement denial.
- Had the Product Owner release the exact fixture hold, tested the independent
  45-minute freshness guard, published fresh local coverage, and performed one

- Updated the [deployment readiness guide](../guides/deployment-runtime-and-backup-readiness.md)
  with the fixture evidence, hold/release boundary, limitations, and remaining

## 2. Resulting Behavior

The application and independent CLI ran as `chuck` (UID/EUID 1000), without
privilege escalation. The operator's root-only helper verified exact device
and inode identities, types, ownership, permissions, non-symlink paths, and no
open protected-file holders before changing the disposable fixture. Held
directories were `root:chuck 0750`; protected authority, selection, database,
and WAL files were `root:chuck 0440`. After the Product Owner-approved
adjustment, only the SQLite SHM was `root:chuck 0660`, permitting online backup
while DB and WAL writes and protected-file replacement remained denied.

Candidate reads, a fresh verified backup, and a distinct local restore
succeeded under the hold. A fresh CLI write failed with
`attempt to write a readonly database`. The actual browser POST also reached
the held writer and failed with that SQLite error; the route surfaced a generic
HTTP 500 `unexpected_error`, not a clear denial message. No candidate event was
added, and the database, WAL, SHM, authority/configuration, selections, and
selected prompt matched their pre-attempt hashes.

After operator release, an injected clock at the inclusive 45-minute cutoff
was rejected by the independent freshness guard with `writes are paused` and
`45.0 minutes old`. A new verified local recovery point then permitted one
candidate-only CLI write. Production remains **NO-GO**.

## 4. Architecture / Integration Notes

- The hold relies on root ownership and non-writable parent directories, not
  on the 45-minute recovery-point guard or same-owner `chmod`. The app identity
  could neither change protected modes nor create a replacement sibling.
- The operator-approved writable SHM exception was necessary for SQLite's
  online backup API. The protected database and WAL remained read-only.
- The browser's InformationStore and lock roots stayed on the candidate. The
  browser fixture redirected only scanner cache output to a separate writable
  local cache because normal startup tried to create scanner temporary files
  under the held root.
- The Product Owner's root-elevated release restored the original fixture
  owners and modes. The application and CLI never ran with root privileges.

  production control.

## 8. Known Limitations

- The browser UI reports `unexpected_error` for this filesystem-level denial.
  A future deployment relying on interactive browser traffic should provide a
  clear, validated denial response.
- A held browser needs a supported writable scanner-cache location; the stock
  startup path was not available under the protected candidate root.
- Evidence is local disposable-fixture evidence only. Production authority,
  NAS access isolation, backup/restore, timers, and live cutover remain
  untested and unauthorized.
