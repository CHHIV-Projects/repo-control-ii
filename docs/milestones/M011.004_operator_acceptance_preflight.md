## Deferred backup-monitor design evidence — not current acceptance scope

The following Option A/B analysis, benchmark, and test-matrix results are retained as historical evidence only. The PO has deferred the optimization from this milestone; no current implementation, live acceptance, policy rollout, or production-readiness claim follows from them.

| Option | Proposed verification guarantee | Cost, failure and recovery implications |
|---|---|---|
| **A — previously recommended; now deferred:** exact recovery-point probe every five minutes; full historical scrub hourly | Every fast probe rehashes the exact published payload and checks existing record/authority/manifest bindings with the unchanged 1,200-second age limit. Historical scrub must complete within ten minutes; its receipt expires at 70 minutes. Old corruption is not necessarily detected by intervening fast probes. | Simplest separation, approximately one catalog read/hour rather than twelve; no metadata-only payload cache. Historical errors fail overall readiness even when latest freshness passes. Missing/expired receipt or a failed/latest-unverified job cannot be green. Full-scrub cache pressure still needs an operator-tested resource budget. |
| **B — rolling scrub:** exact-point probe plus up to 1 GiB of complete historical payloads each five-minute cycle | Six-hour full-catalog recheck deadline while the retained corpus is at most 64 GiB and files fit the work budget. All selected files are fully rehashed; unchanged mtime/size is never proof. Oversize files, catalog growth, failed rounds or overdue entries produce an explicit unresolved state. | Less burst I/O/cache, but needs an atomic private checkpoint bound to authority and immutable manifest digests, fair scheduling, retention/delete handling and restart recovery. Lost/unverifiable checkpoints force revalidation, not assumed success. No retention deletion or cap is authorized. |

These remain design alternatives only; the PO-approved Q7 disposition defers implementation and acceptance in this milestone. Neither silently falls back from a failed exact point to an older one. Canonical SHA-256 checks detect corruption/tampering relative to the retained binding, not an attacker capable of rewriting all trusted evidence; no stronger authenticity claim or new signing system is proposed.

The prototype reuses existing record/payload verifiers and demonstrates a shared publication lock. **It must not be added alone:** the current publisher's nonblocking exclusive lock fails when a reader holds that lock. A regression demonstrates that conflict, then demonstrates a bounded waiting publisher against the same disposable backup implementation. Proposed production budgets are fast probe <=30 seconds and publisher lock wait <=35 seconds, followed by explicit failure/retry, never indefinite waiting. The full historical scrub must not hold the publisher lock for its entire catalog scan. Latest job outcome and durable publication are explicit simulated inputs today; an actual producer-bound status/receipt and timeout integration still need implementation and validation.

Current publication uses fsynced files and ordered database/manifest renames, followed by the local recovery record. The prototype rejects partial/nonfinal manifests and pending publication, but is not a NAS power-loss/directory-durability test. Do not claim final crash-durable publication merely from a timer offset or this fixture.
